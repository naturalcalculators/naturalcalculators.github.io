Complex numbers

How to switch into CMPLX mode, type i, and get answers back in a+bi or polar form.

Switching modes

Press MODE and choose 2: CMPLX. A small CMPLX marker appears in the display and stays there until you switch back with MODE 1. Changing modes clears whatever you had on screen, so finish your current calculation first.

Typing i

The imaginary unit is the red i above the ENG key, so press ALPHA then ENG. Type 2 + 3 then ALPHA ENG to enter 2+3i.

Multiplication is implied, so 3i is three times i, and 2(1+i) works the way you would write it.

Arithmetic

Add, subtract, multiply and divide as normal. Division is the one worth checking, because doing it by hand means multiplying by the conjugate:

You typeYou get
(2+3i) + (1−i)3+2i
(2+3i) × (1−i)5+i
(2+3i) ÷ (1−i)−1⁄2 + 5⁄2 i
(1+i) ÷ (1−i)i
i²−1
1 ÷ i−i

Notice that the parts come back as exact fractions, the same way they do for real answers. Press S⇔D for decimals.

Powers and roots

Whole-number powers work directly — (1+i)² is 2i and (1+i)⁸ is 16. Negative powers give you reciprocals, so (2+3i)⁻¹ is 2⁄13 − 3⁄13 i.

Square roots of negatives work here. In normal COMP mode √−4 is a Math ERROR, because there is no real answer. In CMPLX mode it is 2i.

Modulus, argument and conjugate

Press SHIFT then 2 to open the complex menu:

  • 1: arg — the argument, the angle the number makes in the complex plane. Reported in whatever angle unit you are in, so arg(i) is 90 in degrees and π⁄2 in radians.
  • 2: Conjg — the conjugate. Flips the sign of the imaginary part: Conjg(2+3i) is 2−3i.
  • 3: r∠θ — show answers in polar form.
  • 4: a+bi — back to rectangular form.

For the modulus, use the absolute-value bars: |3+4i| is 5, which is just Pythagoras on the real and imaginary parts.

Polar form

Every complex number can be written either as a+bi (how far along, how far up) or as r∠θ (how far from the origin, at what angle). Switching between them is what SHIFT 2 then 3 and 4 do.

Polar form is the easier one for multiplication and powers — you multiply the lengths and add the angles — which is why it shows up everywhere in electrical engineering and anything involving phase.

What does not work in CMPLX mode

Trigonometric and logarithmic functions refuse complex arguments and return a Math ERROR, matching the hardware this is modelled on. So does the factorial. For those, switch back to COMP mode with MODE 1.
