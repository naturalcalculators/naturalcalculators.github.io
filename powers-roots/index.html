Powers, squares and roots

The nth root: the index goes first

This is the one people get wrong, because it is the only key on the whole keypad where part of the answer is typed before you press it.

For the fourth root of 16, type 4 first, then SHIFT x■, then 16, then =. You get 2. The 4 you already typed becomes the small index sitting in front of the root sign, exactly as it is written on paper.

Type it the other way round — the key first, then 4, then 16 — and you have asked for something else entirely.

The mistake that quietly ruins the answer

When you press x■, the cursor jumps up into a small raised box, and it stays there. Everything you type next is part of the exponent until you press ▶ to come back down to the baseline.

So 2 x■ 3 + 1 is not 9. Without pressing ▶ first you have typed two to the power of four. The display shows it correctly — the +1 is visibly up in the air — but it is easy to type without looking. If a power calculation comes out strangely large or small, this is almost always the cause.

The same applies to √. Everything goes under the bar until you press ▶, so √9+16 is the square root of 25 if you never left the root, and 3+16 if you did. Both are legitimate expressions, which is why the calculator cannot warn you.

Fractional and negative powers

They behave exactly as the algebra says, which makes them a useful shortcut:

  • A power of 1⁄2 is a square root, and 1⁄3 is a cube root. Build the fraction inside the exponent box with the fraction key.
  • A negative power is a reciprocal: 2⁻³ is 1⁄8.
  • A power of 2⁄3 is the cube root, squared.

Answers stay exact wherever possible, so you get 1⁄8 rather than 0.125. Press S⇔D if you wanted the decimal.

Roots of negative numbers

The square root of a negative number is a Math ERROR in normal COMP mode, because there is no real number that squares to give a negative. That is the calculator being correct, not broken. If you are working with complex numbers, switch to CMPLX mode and √−4 becomes 2i.

The cube root of a negative number is a genuine real number — ∛−8 is −2 — so use the cube-root key for it. Writing it as a fractional power instead returns an error, because raising a negative number to a fractional power is not defined in the reals.

Why √8 does not give 2.828

It gives 2√2, which is the same number written exactly. The calculator pulls out every perfect-square factor it can find. There is a whole page on exact answers, but the short version is: press S⇔D for the decimal.
