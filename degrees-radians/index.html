Degrees, radians and gradians

One setting, three modes, and the most common source of a confidently wrong trigonometry answer.

How to switch

Press SHIFT then MODE to open SETUP, then choose:

  • 3 for Deg — degrees
  • 4 for Rad — radians
  • 5 for Gra — gradians

A small D, R or G sits at the top right of the display at all times. Before any trigonometry, glance at it. It takes half a second and it is the difference between a right answer and a confidently wrong one.

What the three actually are

UnitFull circleRight angleUsed for
Degrees36090Geometry, navigation, most school trigonometry
Radians2ππ⁄2Calculus, physics, anything with a derivative in it
Gradians400100Surveying, and very little else

A radian is the angle you get when the arc length equals the radius. It looks like an awkward unit until you differentiate a sine — the derivative of sin x is cos x only when x is in radians. In degrees you pick up a factor of π⁄180 every time, which is why calculus uses radians exclusively.

The mistake

In degrees, sin 30 is exactly a half. In radians, sin 30 is about −0.988, because 30 radians is a little under five full turns.

Both are correct answers to different questions, and the calculator has no way to know which one you meant. Nothing looks wrong — you just get a number that is not the number you wanted.

The rule of thumb. If the question has a degree symbol, or mentions a triangle, use degrees. If it involves π, a limit, a derivative or an integral, use radians. If you are asked for sin of a plain number with no units, it is almost certainly radians.

Converting between them

You rarely need to do this by hand, but the arithmetic is simple:

  • Degrees to radians: multiply by π⁄180.
  • Radians to degrees: multiply by 180⁄π.
  • Degrees to gradians: multiply by 10⁄9.

So 45° in radians is 45 × π ÷ 180, which the calculator returns as π⁄4 — exact, because it keeps multiples of π intact rather than flattening them to 0.7853981634.

Inverse trigonometry works the same way

Where sin takes an angle and returns a ratio, sin⁻¹ takes a ratio and returns an angle — expressed in whatever mode you are currently in. Ask for sin⁻¹(0.5) and you get 30 in degrees, or π⁄6 in radians. Same angle, different unit.

Degrees, minutes and seconds

Angles are sometimes written as degrees, minutes and seconds — 1°30′00″ means one and a half degrees. Use the °′″ key between each part: type 1, the key, 30, the key, 0, the key. The calculator converts it to 1.5 for you.
