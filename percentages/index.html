Percentages

The % key does one small thing. Once you know what it is, every percentage question becomes ordinary arithmetic.

What the % key actually does

Press SHIFT then ( for %. It divides the number in front of it by 100. That is the whole function.

So 15% becomes 0.15, and 200 × 15% gives 30 — fifteen percent of two hundred, which is what you wanted.

But 50 + 10% gives 50.1, not 55. It read that as fifty plus one tenth, because that is literally what it says. Some calculators quietly rewrite + 10% to mean “add ten percent of the first number”. This one does not guess — it does the arithmetic you typed. Once you know that, it is entirely predictable.

The multiplier: the one idea worth learning

Every percentage increase or decrease is a single multiplication, and the multiplier is easy to build in your head:

  • Up 10% → × 1.1. Up 7.5% → × 1.075. Up 200% → × 3.
  • Down 20% → × 0.8. Down 5% → × 0.95.

This is worth the small effort because multipliers chain. A price that goes up 10% and then down 10% is × 1.1 × 0.9, which is 0.99 — you end up slightly below where you started, which surprises people every time. Three years of 4% inflation is × 1.04³, not × 1.12.

Percentage change

To find how much something changed in percent, the formula is the difference divided by the original, times 100:

(new − old) ÷ old × 100

A price going from 40 to 46 is (46 − 40) ÷ 40 × 100, which is a 15% rise. Dividing by the new value instead is the classic mistake and gives 13.04% — close enough to look plausible, which is what makes it dangerous.

Reversing a percentage

If a total already includes a percentage and you want the amount before it, divide by the multiplier rather than subtracting the percentage.

A bill of 96 including 20% tax was 80 before tax: 96 ÷ 1.2 = 80. Taking 20% off 96 instead gives 76.8, which is wrong, because the 20% was calculated on the smaller number.

Keeping the running number

Percentage work often means using one answer several times. After pressing =, the Ans key holds that result, so you can carry straight on: Ans × 1.05 applies another five percent. To park a number somewhere more permanent, use the memory keys.
