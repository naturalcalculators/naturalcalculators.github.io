Guide

How to use a scientific calculator

Every key, in the order you are likely to need it. If you are staring at a keypad wondering what half of it does, start here.

The two keys that change everything else

Almost every key does three jobs. The label printed on the key is what you get by pressing it. The yellow label printed above it is what you get by pressing SHIFT first. The red label is what you get after ALPHA.

So SHIFT then the square-root key gives you a cube root, because that is what is printed above it in yellow. And ALPHA then sin gives you the variable D, because that is the red label. Once that clicks, the keypad stops being mysterious — there are about sixty functions on roughly twenty keys.

A small S or A appears at the top of the display to tell you which one is armed. Press the key again to disarm it.

Entering an expression

You type left to right, exactly as you would write it, and nothing is calculated until you press =. That means you can go back and fix a mistake before committing.

  • ◀ and ▶ move the cursor through what you typed, including into a fraction or a root.
  • ▲ and ▼ jump between a fraction's numerator and denominator. With nothing to jump between, they scroll back through your previous calculations.
  • DEL deletes the character before the cursor. AC clears the whole line.

After you press =, pressing an operator continues from the answer. Type 5 = + 3 = and you get 8, because the calculator inserts Ans for you.

Trigonometry

sin, cos and tan open a bracket for you, so sin(30 is enough — the closing bracket is optional at the end of a line. SHIFT first gives the inverse functions (sin⁻¹ and friends), which return an angle.

hyp is a modifier, like SHIFT: press it, then sin, and you get sinh. Press hyp, then SHIFT, then sin for sinh⁻¹.

Your answer depends entirely on whether the calculator is in degrees or radians. That deserves its own page.

Logarithms

  • log is base 10. ln is base e.
  • log□□ takes any base: type the base, press ▶, type the number.
  • SHIFT log gives 10■, and SHIFT ln gives e■ — the inverses.

Memory

There are nine places to put a number: A to F, X, Y, and an independent M.

  • Store: calculate something, then SHIFT RCL followed by the variable's key.
  • Recall: RCL then the key, or ALPHA then the key to drop the variable into an expression.
  • M+ adds the current answer to M; SHIFT M+ subtracts. Useful for running totals.
  • Ans always holds the last result, whether you stored it or not.

Making the calculator bigger

The − and + buttons under the keypad scale the whole calculator, from 70% up to 200%. Useful on a large monitor, on a phone, or if you are projecting it for a class. The size is remembered the next time you visit.

SETUP

SHIFT MODE opens the settings:

  • 1 — Textbook display. Fractions stack and roots get a bar. This is the default and almost always what you want.
  • 2 — Line display. Everything on one line, decimals instead of exact forms. Occasionally clearer for long expressions.
  • 3, 4, 5 — Deg, Rad, Gra. The angle unit.
  • 6 — Fix. A fixed number of decimal places.
  • 7 — Sci. A fixed number of significant figures, in scientific notation.
  • 8 — Norm. Back to normal rounding.

Fix and Sci change only how the answer is shown. The calculator keeps full precision internally, so chained calculations do not accumulate rounding error.
