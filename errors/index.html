Math ERROR and Syntax ERROR

Two messages cover nearly every error a scientific calculator can produce. Each has a short, boring list of causes.

First: you have not lost your work

When an error appears, press ◀ or ▶. Your expression comes back with the cursor in it, ready to edit. You do not have to retype anything.

AC clears the line completely. Use it only when you actually want to start again.

Syntax ERROR: the expression is not valid

The calculator could not read what you typed. It has not attempted any arithmetic. Almost always one of these:

CauseLooks like
Unbalanced brackets(3+2))×4 — one too many closing brackets
An operator with nothing to work on5 × ÷ 2, or a line ending in +
Two numbers with nothing between themusually a stray digit left behind after editing
A function with an empty bracketsin( )
Minus used as a negative sign−5 typed with the subtraction key where (−) was needed

Extra closing brackets are the most common by a distance, because functions like sin and log open a bracket for you automatically and it is easy to close it twice. You never need a closing bracket at the very end of a line — the calculator supplies them.

Math ERROR: the expression is valid but the answer is not a number

Here the calculator understood you perfectly and tried. There is simply no answer to give.

You asked forWhy there is no answer
1 ÷ 0Division by zero is undefined. This includes dividing by a variable that happens to hold zero.
√−4No real number squares to a negative. Works in CMPLX mode, where it is 2i.
log(−2)Logarithms of zero and negatives are undefined.
tan(90°)The tangent is genuinely infinite there.
sin⁻¹(2)Sine never exceeds 1, so nothing has a sine of 2.
0.5!Factorials are defined for whole numbers only.
(−8)^(1⁄3)A negative to a fractional power is undefined in the reals — use the cube-root key, which gives −2.
Something enormousPast roughly 10³⁰⁰ there is nowhere to put the digits.

The angle-mode trap

If tan(90) errors and you expected a number, check the D or R at the top of the display. In degrees, tan 90° is undefined and the error is correct. In radians, 90 radians is about 14 full turns and gives a perfectly ordinary answer.

The reverse bites harder: in the wrong mode you often get no error at all, just a wrong number. That is worth a page of its own.

Errors that are really typing mistakes

Three specific ones account for most of the confusing cases:

  • Still inside the exponent box. After x■ everything you type stays raised until you press ▶. A whole expression trapped up there produces a number large enough to overflow. More on that here.
  • Still under the root bar. Same idea. Half your expression ends up inside √, and if it turns negative you get a Math ERROR from a calculation that should have been fine.
  • Subtraction sign used as a negative. − needs something on both sides; (−) makes a number negative. They look almost identical on screen.

When there is no error and the answer is still wrong

The genuinely dangerous case, because nothing warns you. Run through:

  • Angle mode — D, R or G in the display.
  • A leftover variable — if the expression uses A or X, is the value in it the one you meant? Recall it and check.
  • Implicit multiplication — 6 ÷ 2(1+2) is 1, not 9, because a number pressed straight against a bracket binds more tightly than a written ÷.
  • A rounding setting — Fix or Sci left on from an earlier problem. SETUP, then Norm, puts it back.
A rough estimate is the best check there is. If you expected about 40 and the screen says 0.04, something structural is wrong, and no amount of retyping the same keys will find it.
