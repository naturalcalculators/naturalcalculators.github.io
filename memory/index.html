Memory: M+, STO, RCL and Ans

Nine places to keep a number, a running total that survives everything else you do, and the answer you already have.

Ans: the memory you are already using

Ans holds the last result, always, whether you asked for it or not. Two things follow from that.

First, starting a line with an operator continues from the answer. Type 5 = then + 3 = and you get 8, because Ans was inserted for you.

Second, you can reuse a result mid-expression without writing it down. √(Ans), Ans × 1.05, 1 ÷ Ans — all fine, and all carrying full internal precision rather than the ten digits shown on screen.

The nine variables

There are nine named places to put a number: A, B, C, D, E, F, X, Y and M. Their names are the red letters printed above the keys.

SHIFT RCL is STO — the yellow label above the RCL key. After it, you press the key the letter sits above, not a letter key: A lives above (−), X above ), M above M+, and so on.

M+ and M−: the running total

M is different from the other eight. It has its own two keys for adding to it and subtracting from it, which makes it the right place for a total you are building up.

  • M+ adds whatever is currently displayed to M.
  • SHIFT M+ is M−, which subtracts it.
  • RCL then M+ brings M back; press = to show it.

So a shopping list is 4 × 2.50 M+, 3 × 1.20 M+, 9.99 M+, then RCL M+ = for the total — 23.59. You never write down a subtotal, and a mistake costs you one line rather than the whole list.

Money totals come back in exact form, so 23.59 may appear as 2359⁄100. Press S⇔D for the decimal.

M keeps its value until you clear it. Pressing AC, turning off, or doing unrelated sums does not touch it. Zero it deliberately: type 0, then SHIFT RCL, then M+. A leftover M from yesterday is a genuinely confusing bug to chase.

Where the letters earn their keep

The other variables are for when a number appears several times in one problem, or when you want to change one input and re-run everything.

Store a radius in A, then type π × A² for the area and 2πA for the circumference — no retyping, no rounding drift. Change A and both are one keypress away again.

CALC: the same idea, automated

Type a formula with letters in it — say A × B ÷ 2 — and press CALC. The calculator asks you for A, then for B, then gives the answer. Press CALC again for a new pair. It is a one-line spreadsheet, and it is the fastest way to run the same formula over twenty sets of numbers.

SOLVE: working backwards

SHIFT CALC is SOLVE. Type an expression in terms of X and it finds the value of X that makes it zero. Enter X² − 4 and SOLVE returns 2.

To solve an equation with something on both sides, move everything to one side first: 3X + 1 = 10 becomes 3X + 1 − 10, and SOLVE gives 3.
